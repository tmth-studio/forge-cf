1 · Your business and objectives

What the business does

Spoke is a mobile bicycle-servicing business. A customer books a slot online; a mechanic arrives in a liveried van and services the bicycle at the door. Two products: a full service at £80 in_product_0_price (band £65 to £90, from Halfords and independent price lists) and a safety check at £25 in_product_1_price (band £20 to £30). Full services are 60% of jobs in_product_0_share.

The business is organised as borough teams. One team serves 100,000 households in_market_unit_population, of which 38% own a bicycle an adult rides in_market_non_viable_share. At 6% penetration in_market_penetration_steady_state that is 2,280 customers customers_per_unit and 456 services a month transactions_per_unit. Greater London supports 34 teams units. At that scale the business turns over £899,232 a month venture_revenue_per_month and returns £2,703,643 a year venture_surplus_per_year after every cost, including the return on capital and tax.

Objectives

Short term (this year)
Open the first borough team by month one and reach 456 services a month transactions_per_unit by month eleven. Sell 3,580 services in the year year1_volume for £207,617 year1_sales. Confirm the full-service price holds at £80 in_product_0_price with real customers by month six — this is the number the whole case rests on.
Medium term (one to two years)
Open team two in an adjoining borough once the first team runs at a margin of safety of 25% or more for three consecutive months. Two teams open at the end of year two in_forecast_units_open_year_2, selling £524,993 forecast_revenue_year_2 at the unit economics on the Unit P&L tab: a surplus of £6,627 per team per month unit_surplus once a team is at steady state.

3 · Your target customers

The customer is a London household with at least one adult who rides regularly and does not want to lose half a day taking the bicycle to a shop. The problem is time, not price: a shop service costs about the same, but it costs two trips, a wait of three to ten days and a bicycle out of use. Spoke removes the two trips and the wait.

The market is bounded from the bottom up: 3,400,000 reachable households in_market_total_reachable_population (ONS 2024), less the 62% with no regular rider in_market_non_viable_share, gives 1,292,000 households tam. A customer buys 0.2 services a month in_market_transactions_per_customer_per_month — two to three a year — the trade norm.

How the prices were set

Prices were set from what customers pay today, then tested against cost. A full service at an independent London shop is £65 to £90. Spoke prices at £80 in_product_0_price, inside that band. The customer's saved time is the extra value and it does not need to be given away. The whole cost of a service is £43.47 cost_per_transaction. That includes parts, staff, the van, the booking platform, start-up costs spread over five years, the return on capital, tax and the share of head office. The average price is £58.00 interim_price. The margin of safety is 33.4% margin_of_safety. The gate is 25%. The price needed to sit exactly at the gate is £54.34 required_price on average: £74.94 required_price_product_1 for a full service and £23.42 required_price_product_2 for a check. There is £8.01 price_fall_to_floor of room before the margin reaches the 15% floor.

What sets Spoke apart

The bicycle never leaves the house. Once a customer has booked twice, the mechanic knows the bicycle and its history sits in the booking record; moving to a shop means starting again. The vans, the routing and the trained team are the asset a shop cannot copy without becoming a mobile business itself. Parts come from two trade suppliers and either can be replaced at a week's notice.

Strengths, weaknesses, opportunities and threats

  • strengths: the time saving is real and visible; the unit economics pass the gate with room
  • weaknesses: the case depends on the full-service price holding at £80 in_product_0_price; penetration is assumed, not measured
  • opportunities: e-bike servicing, which shops are slow to take on; corporate fleets
  • threats: a chain launching a mobile service; a wet spring reducing bookings in the ramp months

5 · Your sales and marketing plans

Customers hear of Spoke three ways: a leaflet drop and local social advertising in the launch borough (£5,000 line_startup_launch_marketing_in_the_borough in the funding table above, within the £45,503 startup_per_unit of start-up cost), a referral credit for existing customers, and partnerships with the borough's cycling clubs and employers with cycle-to-work schemes. The booking coordinator follows up every enquiry: 1.3 contacts per confirmed booking are costed in the staffing hr_cost_per_unit. Head-office marketing spend at scale is £6,000 a month line_ho_running_marketing_spend, inside the £8,700 running_head_office of head-office running cost, and 30% of it is carried in year one in_cashflow_year1_head_office_share.

7 · Back-up plan

⚠ The year-one plan needs £115,461 cash_needed more than the opening balance to stay above zero, and £129,675 cash_needed_stress if revenue is 10% below forecast. Funding in hand is £85,000: the £25,000 loan in_cashflow_loan_amount and £60,000 of equity in_cashflow_other_equity. The gap is £30,461 in the base case and £44,675 under stress.

Three things protect the loan repayment of £483 a month loan_repayment_per_month.

  1. Price can fall £8.01 price_fall_to_floor per service before the margin reaches the 15% floor. The full-service price is the input that moves the margin most binding_input: at the bottom of its band, £65, the margin falls to 12.7% and the case fails. The first six months test that price with real customers before any second team is opened.
  2. If every input lands at its bad end at once the margin is −43.2% margin_of_safety_worst_corner. That is the case for not opening team two until the first team has run three months at the gate. Nothing is committed to a second borough until then.
  3. The gap is closed by delaying the booking platform. Year one runs on an off-the-shelf booking tool at £150 a month; the £60,000 capital_head_office platform is built from the surplus of teams two to four. With that change the plan needs no cash beyond the £85,000 in hand, and the stress case needs £8,000 of it held back as a reserve. Both numbers are read from the Cash flow tab with the platform line set to zero.

Beyond year one, the forecast cash flow on tab 17 is the test. Its lowest year-end balance is the year-one figure, −£108,913 forecast_lowest_closing_cash, so the extra funding the five-year plan needs is £108,913 forecast_funding_needed — the same year-one gap, closed by the platform delay above. If a later round does not come, the plan stops opening teams at the number the cash supports and the head office is held at the share already staffed; the unit economics do not depend on the roll-out.

If the price does not hold, the margin sits below the 15% floor margin_from_failure at month six. Then the business stops opening teams and runs the one van as a lifestyle business. At 456 services a month the one team clears its costs and the loan; that is the realistic floor, and it does not need a second borough to be true.
