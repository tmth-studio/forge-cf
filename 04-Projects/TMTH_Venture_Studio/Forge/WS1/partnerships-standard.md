## 1 · The frame — the partner who is neither payer nor paid

Four facts fix the shape of this standard.

**1. The partner is derived, not imported.** IVE puts the customer's routine at the centre of R7. A gateway partner exists only where it stands in that routine already, at the step where the customer's problem becomes concrete (R7 SR2). So the partner is read off the R7 enumeration, per `derivation-standard.md`. It is not chosen from a list of firms the trade calls channels.

**2. The partner is classed before it is named** (VA-147, 17 September 2026). Every candidate present in the customer's routine sits in one of two classes. **Loss side:** the architecture takes a pool of income from it. **Gain side:** the architecture pays it. R7 prefers the loss side, and prices its case against the pool it stands to lose, never against the status quo. The pool is printed per incumbent in R2's `POOL AT RISK` block.

**3. The partner's return is classed as fee-in-floor or share-of-surplus.** A gateway partner is neither the payer nor a supplier. So its return has no natural home in the unit economics and must be given one. The two forms behave differently, and the record must print both readings:

- **Fee in the floor.** A fixed payment *f* per referral or per unit enters the floor. Unit margin becomes (ceiling − floor − *f*) ÷ (floor + *f*). The fee is paid whether or not surplus exists. It raises the floor, so it re-runs the payer's affordability screen (VA-92).
- **Share of surplus.** A share *s* of the surplus (ceiling − floor) goes to the partner. The unit margin as the payer sees it is unchanged. The venture's own reading becomes (1 − *s*) × surplus ÷ (floor + *s* × surplus). The share is paid only when surplus exists. It dilutes the venture's reading and never touches the payer's screen.

Forge R7 v8 chose the second form: a class of 30 per cent of a print's distributions, non-voting, no cash, allocated yearly pro rata to buyers channelled. The venture's reading at that class is 44.1 · 28.0 · 30.4 · 31.1 · 47.9 by family (⚠ T4 basis). The class may not exceed 0.37 without the legal family's reading falling under the 25 line.

**4. An incumbent can be present collectively and absent singly.** Where the customer is an organisation, the firm at step 2 of its routine is often one of tens of thousands. No single firm holds enough of the pool to be worth a deal. A consolidator, a franchisor, a large regional firm or a trade body holds the class in aggregate, and one agreement channels many. This is the **aggregate-incumbent form**. It changes the sale (a board decides, over months), the hours (a band to 300, not 30) and the count line (20 to 60 partners, not 750). The record must say which form it designs for, and must not read one form's hours for the other.

**The conformity guard, applied to this seat.** The reviewer carries the trade's conventions by training. A partner programme with tiers, a referral fee, a commission table, a partner manager per account, a co-marketing fund, a reseller agreement. None of these is a criterion. Reward a departure that is derived from the record. Forge's partner takes no cash, holds no vote and is allocated by a record. The class share is the only return the pool at risk can fund. Fail a component only where the role cannot execute it as specified.

**Standing constraints this seat inherits.** No cold outreach is ever queued for Tom (Tom, 2 September 2026). No cash leaves the venture for a partner at any size (no-cash rule, 2 September 2026). Neither constraint enters the at-scale architecture; both bind the pilot instance (§9).

## 3 · The shared rubric for Tier-2 checks

Tier-1 checks are mechanical: presence, count, arithmetic, traceability. Tier-2 checks are scored one to five by an independent scorer, never the producer; two scorers must agree; the pass mark is four.

| Level | Anchor |
|---|---|
| 5 | Stated in the component's definition, note or cost line, with a number, an owner and a driver, and the reviewer can reproduce it from the record |
| 4 | As 5, but one figure is a band with no convergence event |
| 3 | Stated, with one of number, owner or driver missing |
| 2 | Asserted in a sentence, with no field behind it |
| 1 | Absent |

These descriptors are drafted for ratification. The rubric is Tom's to ratify; applying it is permitted, changing it is not (README, autonomy boundaries).

## 4 · The acts of the seat, and the hours each takes

The partnerships seat runs eight acts. Each is a routine with a driver, a class (run, manage, hold) and a place in the activity-based operating model. The hours in the record are Forge R7 v8's at-scale figures (§16.6, §17). The seat-hour rate is £50 (record). Where the record gives no figure, this standard estimates and marks the estimate ⚠.

| # | Act | Driver | Hours in the record | This standard's estimate (⚠) | Class | Where it sits in the AOM |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Identify — derive the incumbents at risk in a family and territory from public data | per approach | within 30 h an approach | 4 h ⚠ | run | PartP-2, acquisition |
| 2 | Qualify — appraise the pool at risk on the candidate's own client list; class it (VA-147); screen affordability in two currencies | per approach | within 30 h an approach; pool appraisal £60 inference per approach | 6 h ⚠ | run | PartP-2, acquisition |
| 3 | Approach — the conversations, from first contact to a decision | per approach | within 30 h an approach; close one in three ⚠ | 12 h ⚠ | run | PartP-2, acquisition |
| 4 | Terms — the term sheet from the model set; the class, the allocation basis, the exit terms | per close | within 90 h a close | 12 h ⚠ | run | PartP-2, acquisition |
| 5 | Onboard — the agreement executed; the partner entered on the register; the hand-over to the run routine; the partner brief | per close | within 90 h a close; secretariat 6 h a deal (£300), a separate seat | 12 h ⚠ | run | PartP-2 → CP-1, transition |
| 6 | Run — the partner statement; queries on the allocation; keeping the partner channelling; exceptions | per partner-year | **absent from the record** | 4 h ⚠ | manage | CP-1, operation |
| 7 | Review — the yearly re-read of the partner's case against the pool as it now stands; the half-yearly re-run of the count | per partner-year | **absent from the record** | 2 h ⚠ | manage | CP-1, operation |
| 8 | Exit — lapse on kill, exit at par, a partner leaving, a partner in breach; the register closed | per exit | **absent from the record** | 4 h ⚠ | hold | CP-1, disposal |

**The arithmetic the record must reproduce.** Acts 1 to 3 run three times per close at one in three. So per close: 3 × (4 + 6 + 12) = 66 h, plus acts 4 and 5 at 24 h, gives 90 h. That is the record's figure. At about 33 closes a year (178 live prints, one deal a print central) the acquisition function is 2,970 h, or 1.9 FTE on a 1,600-hour year. In a 120-print ramp year it is 10,800 h, or 6.8 FTE. Both match the record.

**What the record does not carry.** Acts 6 to 8 have no hours in R7 v8. At the mean-firm form, 750 partners × 6 h a partner-year = 4,500 h, or 2.8 FTE — larger than the acquisition function it was reported beside. At the aggregate form, 20 to 60 partners × 6 h = 120 to 360 h, or 0.1 to 0.2 FTE. The two forms differ by an order of magnitude on this line, and the record's 1.9 FTE reads neither. This is finding 1 in §6.

**Which form the hours read.** The 30 h an approach and 90 h a close are the mean-firm form. The aggregate form is a board sale over four to six months (record, §17). Its honest hours are the 180-hour corner (record, §16.6) with a band to 300. A record that counts 20 to 60 aggregate partners and reads 90 h a close has mixed the forms (PA-11).

## 6 · What a failed check becomes, and how this standard's own inputs are disposed

Every failure is classified at the moment the reviewer writes it. Nothing is left open (VA-89).

| Class | Meaning | Cap on the verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Design defect | The architecture can be changed to satisfy the check | None; fix and re-run |
| Unmeasured input | The figure is a band with a named convergence event | Verdict capped PROVISIONAL until the event |
| Build item | A component the record names and has not yet built | Verdict capped PROVISIONAL until built |
| Structural limit | The check cannot be met by any design at this scale (VA-101) | Stated in the FIT record; the verdict carries it |
| Standing constraint | A rule outside the architecture (no cash, no cold outreach for Tom, VA-23) | The check does not run at 6b; the pilot instance carries it |

An unclassifiable failure is a design defect.

1. **The run routine is unsized, and at the single-firm form it is the larger function.** R7 v8 sizes the seat at 1.9 FTE from acquisition hours alone. The choice of form moves the function by an order of magnitude on a line the record does not carry. 2. **The count line and the hours line read different forms.** The record counts 20 to 60 aggregate partners and reads 90 h a close, which is the single-firm figure. Disposal: unmeasured input (PA-07), capped PROVISIONAL, convergence at the first deal's terms as executed.

## 7 · The run record — what a WS1 run must emit for this seat

A run that reaches R7 with a gateway partner emits one block, `PARTNERSHIPS SEAT`, into `outputs/` under `run-record-standard.md`. The 6b review reads this block and nothing else for this seat. The fields, in order:

1. **Enumeration** — every candidate present in the customer's routine, with its class (loss side · gain side) and the sign on its own book. The pool at risk from R2 per loss-side candidate. The selection and its reason (PA-01 to PA-04).
2. **Form** — single firm or aggregate; the pool per single firm and per aggregate; the sale form and its duration (PA-05).
3. **Stated absence** — where no partner is selected: the falsifying test (PA-06).
4. **Partner case** — gain − cost − alternative at central and pessimistic; cost in both currencies (PA-07, PA-08).
5. **Return class** — fee in the floor or share of surplus; both readings with formulae; the ceiling per family; the bearer of the no-surplus year (PA-20 to PA-25).
6. **Term sheet fields** — the eight fields of PA-24, each with a value.
7. **Close-rate TPM** — measurement method, window, band, evidence basis, convergence event, refusal band, trigger (PA-15, PA-16).
8. **Acts and hours** — the eight acts of §4 with driver, hours, class and AOM location; the per-close sum; the per-partner-year sum (PA-37, PA-42).
9. **Count line** — partners needed at scale per family, the derivation, the market denominator, G15 today and at the first year's rate, the convergence event (PA-46, PA-47).
10. **Exit states** — the five states with receipt and register entry (PA-38).
11. **Disposals** — every open input in the block classed under §6, with its event.
12. **Standing constraints** — stated, not applied: no cash to a partner; no cold outreach for Tom; VA-23.

A block with a field missing fails PA-49. A block that carries a founder reading in field 8 fails PA-44.

## 10 · What this standard does not cover

- **The sale to the buyer.** `launch-sales-standard.md`. This standard uses its §2, §3 and §5 for the parts of a partner approach that are a sale.
- **The customer after the sale.** `customer-success-standard.md`. The buyer a partner channels is a customer from the yes onward.
- **The money.** `finance-standard.md`. The allocation record's arithmetic, the distribution and the ledger are finance's; this standard checks that the partner's view reads the same record.
- **The class instrument and the register.** `company-secretary-standard.md`. This standard checks that the fields exist; the secretary's standard checks that the instrument is lawful and filed.
- **Whether the pool at risk is real.** R2's `POOL AT RISK` block and `balm-challenge-2-custom`. This standard reads the pool; it does not derive it.
- **The choice of partner over no partner.** R7 SR2 and VA-147 make that choice. This standard checks that it was made by the rule.
- **Marketing to partners.** `head-of-marketing` owns positioning; this standard checks that the narrative is derived, not how it is presented.
