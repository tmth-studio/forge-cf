How to use an LLM to create a venture architecture

An end-to-end process flow — recut against Simanis & Donohue, Deciphering the Market Creator's Dilemma.
Head of Product · 3 June 2026 · IVE/BALM + VA-1–4 discipline + the Market Creator's Dilemma
The governing idea

Architecture is a control task, not a writing task — a generate → evaluate → gate loop over external state, with judgement held separate from generation.

But Simanis sharpens what the loop optimises for: not "satisfy each requirement well" but the fewest moves that satisfy the most requirements while breaking the conventional CBA. Synergy is the moat; the circumvented CLO is the spine.

What changed in this cut (reading Simanis)
  • CLO is its own phase and the seed — identified before the loop; everything productizes around its workaround, not treated as "R1 of 10."
  • Synergy moved into generation — generate for the move that solves the most requirements at once. Convergence becomes confirmation, not repair (the Calmly non-convergence was a generation defect, not a missing cleanup step).
  • Conformity guard on the critic — reward "wrong because it breaks the conventional CBA"; kill only "wrong because incoherent." Otherwise verification regresses toward plausible-but-faulty.
  • Customer-research firewall — pain and value-ceiling in; solution-shape out. Following the customer into the form factor imports the faulty CBA.
  • Built to hold — verification weights the F3 holding architecture; a venture is designed to hold a position, not flip.
What changed in v6 → v7 — the objective function, and validation by launch (16 Sep 2026, Tom's instruction, under the delegation)

The CF now has an objective. It maximises the expected net present value of the venture at the required rate of return over the investment period, subject to the gates (objective-function.md). Until v7 the loop optimised for clearing gates and generating synergy; a gate is a constraint, and the loop had nothing to rank two gate-clearing designs by. Three changes follow. Phase 2 step 4 (Generate) ranks candidate moves by the NPV band they move, not only by requirements served. Phase 3 (Converge) does not close without an E[NPV] from the financial simulation over time, as a band with its binding input named — the fit gate's one-scale-state margin is a constraint check, not the objective. The TPM record gains an NPV-sensitivity field, and it is what orders validation threads, design attention and verification effort.

Validation is by launch. validation-standard.md: every unknown is classified — a designed output is a claim validated at launch, never a question for the customer; a fact in the world takes the desk route first; a person is asked only to decide or to correct. Where launch is cheap, the F2 architecture stood up as the minimum front is the validation instrument; realisation-standard.md Step 3b orders that front in threads by expected NPV narrowed per pound. Phase 5 accordingly puts three things and only three to a human: the PCO, decisions, and corrections.

What changed in v5 → v6 — role review: each function's planned activity is reviewed by the role that will run it (15 Sep 2026, Tom's delegation)
  • Why: the operational limb of the three-check close (restored 4 Sep) had a question but no competent reviewer. On Datum R3 the role-support check returned FAIL and the challenge passed. Nobody with the competence of the role that would run the activity was asked whether it could be run.
  • Canon anchor. Systems engineering already has this construct: specialty engineering (INCOSE SE Handbook 4th ed., ch. 10 — reliability, producibility, supportability, human systems integration, affordability and the rest), integrated through the product team and signed off by discipline at the technical reviews (IEEE 15288.2 / MIL-STD-1521B: SRR, PDR, CDR, TRR, each with entry and exit criteria). A venture's specialty disciplines are sales, marketing, operations, finance, legal, customer success. The CF was missing the review; it was not missing the idea.
  • Fix 1 — step 6b, role review, between Productize and FIT. Every AOM component carries a dri: role (10 Sep rule). After the models are written and before the FIT gate, each role reviews the activities under its seat against its craft standard (e.g. Head of Sales against launch-sales-standard.md, Head of Marketing against landing-page-standard.md). The question is executability: can this role run this activity at this cost and volume, and what does it need that the plan does not give it? Findings are disposed under VA-89 — design defect · standing constraint · build item. Output is findings, never rewrites. A role with no craft standard reviews from general practice and reports "standard missing" — a gap in the standards library, logged.
  • Fix 2 — the conformity guard applies to the reviewer. A specialist carries the conventional CBA by training. The reviewer is told to reward "breaks how this function is conventionally done" and to fail only "cannot be executed by this role as specified". Without this the review pulls the architecture back to convention.
  • Fix 3 — three firings. Per challenge on the components that challenge added or changed (light); a full cross-function pass at Phase 3 Converge once the BFF is fixed; and the specification review in the realisation pipeline (realisation-standard.md step 6b). The full pass is where handoffs between functions are checked; the per-challenge pass cannot see them.
What changed in v3 → v5 — model-build unskippable; conventional model sharpened; CLO found via broken boundary conditions
  • Why: the first autonomous F1 run (3 Jun) followed v2 but built no CTM, no AOM, no fin-sim. It read the BALM skills and skipped their model-build sections — so the FMOS/FIT figures (98–99%) were narrative estimates, not the output of a bottom-up AOM → fin-sim model, and nothing chained from one challenge to the next. The "design loop" wasn't a loop.
  • Fix 1 — fire the skills, write the artefacts. Productize (step 6) is no longer "update CTM + AOM" in prose. It fires /ive-ctm-custom → /ive-aom-custom → /ive-fin-sim-custom and produces real model files. Build the models, never describe them.
  • Fix 2 — FIT runs on the model. The gate (step 7) reads the fin-sim's actual FMOS via /ive-fit-verifier-custom. A FMOS not produced by the model is invalid and fails the gate by default.
  • Fix 3 — the chain IS the models. Step 1 (Retrieve) now reads the current CTM/AOM/fin-sim before designing. Each challenge updates the same model files; the next starts from them. The Design Record is the carrier, not a write-only archive.
  • Fix 4 — the CLO needs a conventional model to live in. Phase 1 now states the conventional commercialisation model first — how this CF would typically be made, sold, delivered, paid for — and finds the CLO within it. You cannot locate the operation that drives cost without first naming the model whose cost it drives. (This is also where R1 ≠ CF bites: the conventional model is the CF commercialised the old way; the CLO is its cost bottleneck, and the workaround circumvents that — it is not "use the CF.")
  • Fix 5 (v4) — for a Path-A technology CF, the conventional model is the default commercialisation of the capability. SR1 asks how the CF would conventionally be productised — for an expertise-as-automation CF that is the TurboTax / LegalZoom / SaaS pattern, not how incumbents deliver the outcome. So "the obvious productisation of your tech" is the conventional baseline, never the workaround. Test: if your R1 workaround is what a smart team would build by default with this technology, it is the conventional model — go a level deeper for the CLO. (Forge, 3 Jun: "TurboTax for venture architecture" was mistaken for the innovation; it is the baseline. The real CLO is the central codification/maintenance of the standard inside that model.)

The process flow

Frame → state the conventional model & find the CLO within it (the spine) → architect around it generating for synergy → converge to confirm → verify independently → sign off. External design record throughout; judgement separate from generation; humans only at the irreducible points.

The seven design decisions — Simanis-faithful

  1. Synergy is the generative objective, not a late check.Generate for the move that solves the most requirements at once — Airbnb harnessing the host's home solved R1, R2 and R3 in one stroke. "That architectural synergy is the real moat." Optimise for it throughout and coherence becomes a by-product; convergence confirms rather than repairs.
  2. The CLO is the spine — identified first, everything productizes around it."The CLO is the first thing to identify. Before any other design work." The whole form factor exists to circumvent it. The workaround that "looks wrong by conventional standards" is the seed the other requirements build on — not the first of ten equal passes. But the CLO can only be found inside a stated conventional model: first name how the CF would conventionally be made, sold, delivered and paid for, then locate the operation within it that disproportionately drives cost. Skip that and you have no baseline to find a CLO against — and you slide toward "the CF is the answer." And for a Path-A technology CF, the conventional model is the default commercialisation of the capability (the expertise-as-software / platform pattern) — so the obvious productisation of your tech is the baseline, not the innovation. Test: if your workaround is what a smart team would build by default with the tech, it is the conventional model — go a level deeper.
  3. Generation ≠ judgement — and guard the judge against conformity bias.An independent critic must score, never the producer. But the critic carries a conformity bias that will mark down the unconventional architecture that's correct. Its rubric must keep "wrong because it breaks the conventional CBA" (the signal it's working) and kill only "wrong because incoherent." Without that, adversarial verification quietly regresses toward plausible-but-faulty.
  4. Firewall customer research."Customer research confirms pain. It doesn't design architecture." Pain and the value ceiling may bound the problem; they must never shape the form factor. Following the customer into the solution is how you import the faulty CBA.
  5. State lives in the artifact, not the context window.The design record (BFF, CTM, AOM, fin-sim) is the source of truth every step reads and writes. This is how a 10-requirement architecture stays coherent when it's far too big to hold in one context. The LLM operates on the file, not on its memory of the file.
  6. Gate on money continuously.FIT runs after every requirement, not at the end. A requirement can't close until the architecture still pays back. Beautiful-but-unviable designs die early, when they're cheap to fix.
  7. Built to hold — and humans only at the irreducible judgements.Weight the F3 holding architecture: a venture is designed to hold a defensible position, not flip. And reserve human judgement for the three points that need it — confirm the PCO, change the objective/gates, final sign-off. Everything between is autonomous. That boundary makes the CF a capability, not a co-pilot.
  8. The models ARE the design — and the chain.Each challenge fires the model skills (CTM, AOM, fin-sim) to produce real artefacts, and the next challenge reads them — that is what makes it a loop rather than ten essays. A FIT verdict not produced by the fin-sim is fiction. The first F1 run (3 Jun) skipped the model build and fabricated its FMOS; this decision exists so that never recurs. The model is not the write-up of the design — it is the design, and it is the only thing that carries between challenges.
The deepest point
A plausible architecture and a sound one look identical on the page. What separates them is whether each move was chosen for cross-requirement leverage against a circumvented CLO — and whether the judge rewarded the design for looking conventionally wrong. Build the loop, choose for synergy, and protect the answer that breaks the rules.
