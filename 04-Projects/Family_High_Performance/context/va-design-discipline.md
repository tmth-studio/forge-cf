## The disease this cures

A 10-challenge process has two opposite failure modes, plus a root cause that produces both:

- **Parts-bin (VA-1):** ten bolted-on strategies that never cohere into one business.
- **Premature design (VA-2):** detail specified before a challenge earned it — false certainty.
- **Justification mode (VA-3, the root cause):** challenge 1 conceives the Business Form Factor, and every later challenge merely *explains how the already-conceived BFF solves it too*. The BFF is defended, never beaten. Observed live in a venture design record. `[evidence: VA-3]`

When challenges defend rather than mutate, you get premature design and false coherence. Fix the root (VA-3) and the other two become checks rather than rescues.

---

## The four principles

### VA-3 — challenges mutate the BFF, they don't justify it (root cause)

The BFF is a **living draft each challenge has standing to rewrite** — not a fixed artifact each challenge must defend. Challenges are mutation pressure, like selection: each round can change the organism, not just describe it. The current BFF is the *baseline to beat* on that challenge's dimension.

**The tell:** if a challenge produces **zero candidate changes to the BFF**, it has defaulted to justification. A genuine challenge always generates alternatives — some of which it then consciously rejects.

**The five-step structure for every challenge:**
1. **Baseline, honestly.** Where does the *current* BFF stand on this challenge's dimension? Not zero, not solved.
2. **Generative target.** "How could we change the BFF to do materially better on this dimension than the baseline?"
3. **Generate ≥2–3 candidate BFF mutations.** Structural changes to the business, not features bolted on. (Zero mutations = the tell above — say so explicitly.)
4. **Evaluate each against the whole.** Gain on this dimension vs cost to coherence (VA-1) and to dimensions earned in earlier challenges. A mutation that maximises one dimension but breaks the synthesis is rejected.
5. **Decide and record.** Adopt a mutation (restate the evolved BFF) or hold the current one with explicit reasons each alternative was rejected — never "it already works."

**Supporting move — under-specify the challenge-1 / PCO BFF.** A fully-formed early BFF anchors everything after it into justification mode. Keep the first cut minimal; license every later challenge to rewrite it. A heavy early BFF is itself premature design (VA-2).

### VA-1 — synthesis test (anchor the BFF to a real business)

Whenever the (possibly evolved) BFF is stated, anchor it to a *real, existing business*: "this now looks like **[real business]**, because **[the mechanism they share]**." If you can name a real business the venture resembles, the parts have cohered. If you *can't*, that's the flag — it's still a parts-bin. Apply at the close of each challenge.

### VA-2 — premature-design audit (traceability)

Every specified detail should trace back to a challenge that *required* it. Anything specified with no such trace is the suspect for premature design. Run via the verification agent (`/verify-venture-custom`, Pass 3) across the whole VDR, and as a light check at each challenge close: "did I just specify something this challenge didn't need?"

### VA-4 — show your working (auditable choices)

The candidates generated and rejected in VA-3 steps 2–4 are not scratch work. Record them in a "considered / not chosen" block so every choice is auditable — a reviewer can see the options weighed, not just the verdict. This is free if VA-3 runs: the generation *is* the working.

---

### VA-80 — no forward references (earn the mechanism)

A challenge is solved with what exists at that challenge: the PCO and the solutions of the challenges before it. A later challenge's actor, mechanism or vocabulary may not close a gap here. A condition that cannot be met yet is a residual carried to the later requirement by number, with nothing said about how it is solved there. A cell that names something the architecture has not yet introduced is not complete; it is borrowing. `[evidence: VA-80]`

### VA-81 — credibility mechanisms: receiver, instrument, supplier

An endorsement, signal, warranty, certification or published record exists to change what a named actor believes. Specify it as that: who must believe what, to decide what; what they rely on today to believe things of that kind; whether the element is that instrument or one they already accept; and which organisation issues it today, from public record, before the element is admitted. Re-derive it whenever a later challenge changes the receiver. Provider properties ("independent", "standing") are never enough on their own. `[evidence: VA-81]`

## How they relate

| Principle | The question it asks | When |
|-----------|----------------------|------|
| VA-3 | Did the challenge *try* to redesign the BFF, or just defend it? | During each challenge (the root) |
| VA-1 | Have the parts *cohered* into one business? | At each challenge close |
| VA-2 | Was each specified detail *earned*? | Standalone audit + light check |
| VA-4 | Are the *options behind each choice* visible? | Output of every choice |

VA-2 (every detail traces to a challenge) + VA-4 (every choice shows its options) together make the whole VDR inspectable.

Restores the three-way close the method had before the verdict block was reduced to the FMOS (Tom, 4 September 2026). `[evidence: VA-91]`

If any limb fails the verdict is FAIL, whatever disposition its failed check carried. **A disposition explains why a check failed. It does not make the check pass.** `[evidence: VA-89]`

> **VA-97 — the financial limb's two halves must be shown independent before both are reported.** The margin of safety and the binding gate are two checks only if they measure different things. Express both from the same inputs; **where one reduces to a transform of the other, say so, report them as one check, and state what an independent second check would have to measure.** A limb printing two numbers that are one number under a transform is the single-number failure VA-91 exists to stop, wearing the fix's clothes. `[evidence: VA-97]`

> **VA-100 — a ruling that has not been landed in an executable check has not been made.** Ratifying a rule is not finished when it is written down. **Every ratification names, in the same pass, where the rule is enforced** — a validator or generator that refuses · a skill's output contract or hard gate · a regression check — **or records that it is a judgement rule no check can carry, and why.** A rule recorded in prose and enforced nowhere can be made, cited, and entirely inert. **Two confirmed instances:** a market-partition ruling was silently disabled by an unrelated file-set instruction because it lived in a skill's body rather than its output contract (VA-83); and the partner business-case ruling sat in three documents for five days while the validator still demanded the field the ruling abolished (VA-99). **The tell in both: the rule was cited approvingly in records written while it was being broken.** **Enforcement:** contractual — regression R-W13, the criteria-registry row "Enforcement point named", and the ratification step in the Head of Product process; it binds the ratification pass, not a run. `[evidence: VA-100]`

> **VA-102 — every modelled actor's journey opens where the actor is not yet in it, and a named absence names its substitute.** The four fixed Become states (Tom, 4 September 2026) put arrival at the front of the CUSTOMER's journey. They bind no other actor. `user_tracks` and `partner_tracks` inherit the schema, the person-state register and the narrative rule, and inherit nothing about where a track opens — so a partner track may open on a partner already at the table and nothing objects. **Three clauses.** (1) **Every actor track opens at arrival** — a state in which the actor does not yet know the venture exists — or carries a recorded reason for not doing so. The customer's four states are the reference form; a non-customer track may compress them and may not skip arrival silently. (2) **A deliberate absence names its substitute.** Where a transition carries no product by decision, the record names the activity that does the work instead, and that activity enters the resource model with a driver. **An activity named in prose and performed by a person is in the operating model, or it is not in the venture; assigning it to the principal moves it to a resource nothing sizes.** (3) **Every resource-model driver that scales with a count of counterparties names the routine and the transition that acquires them** — the 4 September loop rule read in the direction it was not read in. **The rule asks a question and forbids no answer:** "we have no acquisition activity, by design, and here is the mechanism that replaces it" passes, provided the mechanism is named and its cost is driven. Generality test, three seats in one day (7 September 2026), recorded in the evidence file. **The abuse test:** a venture may not dispose of arrival as not-applicable for an actor whose participation the architecture asserts. If the design fails when that actor declines, the actor arrived somehow, and the model says how. **Enforcement:** executable, `_check_track_arrival` in `validate_model.py` (clause 1); regression R-CTM9 (clause 1) and R-W15 (clause 3); clause 2 is contractual, the operating model's activity list. Evidence: VA-102-E. `[evidence: VA-102]`

> **VA-99 — a partner carries a business case, not a key monetizable cost.** A KMC asks what loss an actor already carries. **A partner is not buying relief from a loss; it is deciding whether supplying us beats the next-best use of the same capacity.** So an enabler actor states: its **outcome** (first person) and a **business case** — what supplying us **earns** it, what it **costs** it, **why that beats its next-best use** in its own numbers, how it **fits its operation**, and **what it earns and when.** A commodity vendor carries none of these. **Asking a partner for a KMC produces an unanswerable field, and an unanswerable field gets carried unanswered** — which is what happened for weeks across five partners on one venture. Enforced in the validator from 1 September 2026; models dated earlier warn. (Tom's ruling, 1 September 2026.) **Clarified 14 September 2026 (Head of R&D, on a Head of Verification question):** *dated* means last materially edited, not the header. A model edited after 1 September is a post-1 September model whatever its header says, and a header left behind on an edited file is a date used as a switch. A record re-issued on a model that still warns carries the warning as an open item with an owner. Enforcement: contractual until the checking script compares the header date with the file's edit history. `[evidence: VA-99]`

> **VA-101 — a fourth disposition: the structural limit.** *(Renumbered from VA-96 on 6 September 2026: a principal ruling had already claimed that number the same day. The earlier claim stands.)* VA-89's three dispositions cannot hold a failed check that fails on **a limit of the world rather than of the design**. The standing example: a counterparty's free decision to transact cannot be derived. That is not a design defect (no design removes it), not a standing constraint (the principal did not set it), and not a build item (no capability closes it) — so "a check you cannot classify is a design defect" would **fail every derivation-based venture at C1 for a reason true of all ventures**. A structural limit caps the verdict at **PROVISIONAL** and requires three things: the residual **named**, **bounded** (how much of the venture's value rests on it), and paired with **the validation that addresses it** — because the un-printable residue *is* the validation target. **The test that stops this becoming the new "consciously accepted with conditions": could any competitor, with any budget, remove it? If yes, it is a design defect.** It must be true of every venture in the class, not merely of this one. **Enforcement:** contractual — regression R-W11 and the criteria-registry row "Structural-limit disposition"; the classification itself is a judgement the verdict block records. `[evidence: VA-101]`

> **VA-95 — the verdict composes forward; you cannot fail one requirement and pass the next.** The business form factor accumulates, so a verdict is a statement about the **cumulative architecture** at that point, not about the requirement in isolation. **No requirement carries a verdict better than the worst verdict of any requirement it builds on.** A downstream requirement may repair an upstream failure, but only by naming the specific failed check it closes and showing the arithmetic on the cumulative state; the upstream verdict is annotated, never overwritten. **The test that stops this becoming an escape route: would the failed check still fail if run against the cumulative state at the downstream point?** If yes, it is not repaired. A function gate reads the cumulative verdict at its last challenge — not an average, and not the last requirement taken alone. VA-89 and VA-91 defined the verdict for one challenge and said nothing about composition, which in a cumulative method makes the verdict meaningless. (Tom, 4 September 2026.) `[evidence: VA-95]`

> **VA-92 — test whether an X exists before designing for one, and admit an internal holder.** Every requirement that asks *who is the X* runs an existence test before it enumerates candidates, and admits three answers: an external holder, activated · **an internal holder the architecture has already activated** (where this is the answer, the work is to make its output portable to the next customer, not to recruit it) · a stated absence on falsifiable tests with the arithmetic published, redirecting to the question that is live instead. **State the maximum the architecture can pay any holder of the role, from the venture's binding gate, before naming a candidate.** A run following the form literally when no X exists will either manufacture one or record the requirement as not applicable, and both lose the requirement's value. Two requirements on one venture reached the right answer only by working against the skill's framing. `[evidence: VA-92]`

> **VA-84 — drive the model across every gate; do not compute one threshold and call it the pivot trigger.** A gate found from a single formula is a guess about which constraint binds. Build the check cell, run the model against every gate, report the one that fails first and the margin to it. Where the margin is a small fraction of the assumed value, the margin is the finding and is reported unsoftened. `[evidence: VA-84]`

> **VA-85 — challenges that share the cumulative business form factor do not close in parallel.** Sequence them from the dependency matrix. Where a parallel run is instructed, it may not close its gate on a shared input and must declare that slot OPEN. (Two failures in two days, 2–4 September 2026: 22 findings resolved in parallel produced ten fresh contradictions of one kind; R3 declared its C2 slot OPEN correctly and the damage was one sentence.)

> **VA-83 — the market partition is a validated data file, and the requirement lives in the output contract.** R2 does not close until the TAM model file exists and validates. No instruction about which files a run may write overrides it; where one appears to, the instruction is wrong. A ruling that lives only in a person's memory is one badly-worded brief away from being lost.

> **VA-88 — a long production run writes incrementally.** Create the output file with its section headings before reading the sources; save after each section. Work on disk survives an interrupted run; work held in context does not.

> **VA-82 — classify every item you carry out.** Three kinds, two legitimate: **forward-referenced** (a later challenge owns it — name the requirement by number and nothing else) · **unmeasured input** (the design is complete and rests on a figure, capability or decision nobody holds — legitimate only with an owner, a convergence event and a falsifiable pivot trigger) · **unowned design defect** (no later challenge owns it and continuing will not answer it — it blocks until a closing mechanism is named). An unclassified open-items table lets a failing architecture read as complete.

> **VA-90 — the run does not grade itself.** A requirement is closed provisionally at most until an independent verification artefact for it exists in the venture folder. Run the FIT verifier per requirement; run the consistency audit and the venture verification after F1 and after R10.

> **VA-1 — synthesis test.** When the (possibly evolved) BFF is stated, anchor it: "this now looks like [real business], because [shared mechanism]." If no clean analogue exists, flag it — the parts have not yet cohered.

> **VA-103 — the juggling question, made mechanical: re-verify upstream on every BFF move; full cumulative re-run at each function gate.** Canon reconceives the form factor after every theory of change and asks which BFF best supports all of them so far (Simanis, Manuel et al., *Business Architecture Framework*, 2023, p.10); solving a system's functions sequentially is the failure the method names (*Core Business Archetype*, 2024, p.10). The observed defect was not the ten-per-requirement granularity — both granularities are canon, six-per-requirement in 2023 and three-per-function in 2024 — but that a requirement's pass was treated as standing after the BFF it was passed on had moved. **Three clauses.** (1) Every requirement's synthesis step states what changed in the BFF. (2) Every earlier requirement's named checks and FIT are re-run on the BFF as it now stands, one row each, marked unchanged / re-verified / FAILED; a FAILED row is disposed under VA-89 and caps the verdict under VA-95. (3) At each function gate (C3, C7, C10) every earlier requirement is re-run in full on the cumulative BFF, and the consistency audit and venture verification run there — VA-90's trigger list gains "after F2". **The direction rule that stops this becoming justification mode:** arrows point inward (challenges move the BFF) until nothing moves; only then may the BFF be said to explain the challenges (VA-3 restated positively). A requirement whose last verification predates the last BFF move is not closed. **Decided against the alternative** of switching the CF to three per-function loops: it would re-import the defect the deterministic hardening removed (later-step questions pulled into earlier steps) and would let intra-cluster drift persist for up to four challenges. **Enforcement:** contractual — the VA-103 table in every challenge skill's output block (a blank table is a FAIL); regression R-W16. (Tom, 8 September 2026: "u decide".) Evidence: VA-103-E. `[evidence: VA-103]`

> **VA-133 (formerly RD-014; cited as RD-014 in `validate_model.py`, its export and fork copies, and one venture's customer-journey model and generated views, until those are re-sealed) — a model element a renderer cannot draw is refused at the check, not discovered in the picture.** Every CTM renderer walks the state list in listed order and draws only transitions between neighbouring states. A transition that skips a state was therefore dropped in silence: no error, no warning, and the only symptom a hexagon count that failed to rise. **The rule: a transition between non-neighbouring states is an ERROR in `validate_model.py`, with two legal repairs — reorder the states so the endpoints are adjacent, or declare it as a branch, which the renderer draws explicitly.** The general form, which is what makes this method rather than a bug report: **where a generator and a model can disagree about what exists, the check decides, and silence is never the answer.** Generality test recorded in the evidence file. **Enforcement:** executable, `_check_renderable_transitions` in `validate_model.py` (its message string still reads RD-014 until re-sealed). `[evidence: VA-133]` (formerly `[evidence: RD-014]`)

> **VA-134 (formerly RD-015; cited as RD-015 in the dated messages of 7 and 8 September 2026, which stand as written) — a component owns every act on the critical path, including the acts only the founder can do.** A venture is **not design-complete** if its critical path depends on an act only the principal can perform and no component in the operating model owns that act. Naming the owner as the principal does not discharge this: it moves the work to a resource nothing sizes, nothing costs and nothing can staff at scale. Either a component owns the act, with a driver, a volume and a rate, or the design states plainly that the venture's capacity is bounded by one person's hours and carries that bound into the financial gate. **The abuse test:** if the design fails when that person is unavailable, the act is on the critical path and the model says who does it. > **VA-135 (formerly RD-016; cited as RD-016 in the dated messages of 7 and 8 September 2026, which stand as written) — the available-hours figure is a named input with a source and a date, carried by the financial simulation, never restated in a record.** Every design record in the studio divided by the same assumed annual figure. The measured figure was less than half of it, and on at least one venture the change moves the hurdle enough that a design which cleared its gate no longer clears it. **Three clauses.** (1) The available-hours figure is a single studio input with a stated source and a measurement date. (2) The financial simulation carries it; a record that restates it is restating an input it does not own. (3) **A record that states a capacity figure it does not then compute on is a finding**, not a rounding note — the same defect VA-91 names for headline figures, applied to the divisor. Figures and the generality test are in the evidence file. **Enforcement:** contractual — the financial simulation carries the input; the record check in VA-91 catches a stated figure not computed on. `[evidence: VA-135]` (formerly `[evidence: RD-016]`)

> **VA-136 (formerly RD-017; cited as RD-017 in `fit_margin.py` and `test_fit_margin.py` until those are re-sealed) — a per-unit figure names the scale state of its numerator and of its denominator, and they are the same state.** An activity-based cost base is not portable across scale states. The same model at the same rates, rebuilt at launch drivers instead of at-scale drivers, moves a fixed base and a per-unit figure by large and different factors. The defect has been observed in both directions on the same day. **The rule is one sentence and it is mechanically checkable:** every per-unit figure states the scale state of the numerator and the denominator, and a figure whose two states differ is an ERROR, not a note. **Consequence for gates:** a launch decision may not rest on an at-scale per-unit floor, and an at-scale gate may not be priced on a launch-state mix. Where a price is a rule rather than a level, the at-scale gate applies the rule to a **modelled at-scale mix carried as a band**, and reports the worst modelled unit as the binding case beside it — the observed mix is a portfolio report, not a gate. Figures and the generality test are in the evidence file. **Enforcement:** executable, the scale-state refusal in `fit_margin.py`, tested by `test_fit_margin.py` (message strings still read RD-017 until re-sealed). `[evidence: VA-136]` (formerly `[evidence: RD-017]`)

> **VA-137 (formerly RD-018; cited as RD-018 in one run output under `Forge/WS1/runs/` dated 16 September 2026 and in the dated messages of 7 and 8 September 2026, which stand as written) — every function in the operating model appears as at least one activity in the cost model, or carries a stated reason why it does not; and a derived rate scales with the driver it was derived from.** Two clauses, one root cause: an activity-based build understates cost by exactly the activities its list omits, and nothing inside the build can detect that. (1) **Coverage.** A function with zero activities is not a low rate; it is an omission, and it can be worth more per unit than every overhead line together. Checkable from the two files. (2) **Derivation persistence.** A per-unit rate that was derived by converting a per-event cost through an incidence rate must scale with that rate; held flat, a recorded pivot trigger reads more headroom than its own line detail gives. **The general form: a derived figure carries its derivation, or it is an assumption presented as a derivation.** Figures and the generality test are in the evidence file. **Enforcement:** contractual — clause 1 is checkable from the operating model and the cost model and has no executable check yet; clause 2 is a judgement rule on the derivation record. `[evidence: VA-137]` (formerly `[evidence: RD-018]`)

> **VA-138 (formerly RD-019; cited as RD-019 in the dated messages of 7 and 8 September 2026, which stand as written) — the margin of safety is read as a band, and a band whose sign changes across it has no central case to judge.** The 60 per cent bar is calibrated against the average cost overrun on complex projects (62 per cent, Flyvbjerg and Gardner 2023) — an overrun measured *against the estimate actually made*. So the bar is judged at the **central** case; stressing every input to its pessimistic value and then demanding 60 per cent on top counts the overrun twice. The worst-case stress test is a **second and separate** gate with a lower bar: does the venture survive three major assumptions resolving badly together. Two numbers, two bars, both printed, neither substituted for the other. **The clause that decides the hard cases:** where the band's sign changes across it — negative at one end and positive at the other — there is no central case, because the mean of a sign-changing band is a statement about the model's ignorance and not about the venture. That result is not a pass at the central and not a fail at the pessimistic; **the gate is unreadable, the verdict is return-to-narrow, and the work is to identify the single input driving the span.** Generality test recorded in the evidence file. **Enforcement:** contractual, the financial limb of the challenge verdict block (VA-91); the margin script computes one band and does not yet test its sign. `[evidence: VA-138]` (formerly `[evidence: RD-019]`)

> **VA-139 (formerly RD-020; cited as RD-020 in the dated messages of 7 and 8 September 2026, which stand as written) — one margin of safety per payer route, and a route that cannot clear is removed from the architecture rather than carried as an option.** The price ceiling is derived from the key monetizable cost of the actor who **pays**. An architecture with more than one funding route has more than one payer, therefore more than one ceiling, therefore more than one margin. **Computing a single margin against one actor's willingness to pay, on a route where that actor pays nothing, is a category error — the route has been priced against a payer who is not on it.** So: an FMOS per supported payer route, mandatory, not a refinement. **The gate is the route the launch depends on. Every other supported route must clear the survival bar, because a route that exists in the architecture is a route some customer will choose, and a losing route the venture can be pushed onto is a defect, not an option.** A route that cannot clear comes out of the design. Generality test recorded in the evidence file. **Enforcement:** contractual, the financial limb of the challenge verdict block (VA-91), one margin line per supported payer route; no executable check yet. `[evidence: VA-139]` (formerly `[evidence: RD-020]`)

> **VA-140 — an activity's volume driver is a reference to a named quantity, never a sentence (16 September 2026; Head of R&D, RD-031; object-after 18 September 2026 17:00 BST).** An operating model computes cost from activities, and every activity scales with something. The question the rule asks of every activity row is: *what counted thing does this row scale with?* The answer is written as a reference the checking script resolves.
